## 1. Purpose and career fit

Office, retail and other commercial property is valued lease by lease: each tenant has its own rent steps, reimbursement structure, expiry date and rollover risk. Analysts at developers, lenders, brokerages and investment managers are expected to (a) read a rent roll and a lease abstract, (b) understand what lease-by-lease software does with them, and (c) sanity-check its output. Interview tests ask candidates to explain a recovery structure, compute a net effective rent, or say why a cash flow dips in a rollover year. This category makes the student able to rebuild the software's arithmetic in a spreadsheet, which is the only reliable way to audit it.

It complements the core Excel course, which is built on multifamily (one rent line, one vacancy rate). It may reuse Excel skills freely and must teach any Excel it needs.

## 2. Learning objectives

Each objective is measurable and maps to subtopics.

1. Given a rent roll, the student can identify each column's meaning and compute occupancy, weighted average rent and weighted average remaining lease term. (`argus-rentroll`)
2. Given lease language or an abstract, the student can extract commencement, expiry, area, base rent schedule, escalations, recovery structure, options and concessions. (`argus-rentroll`)
3. Given a base rent and escalation terms, the student can compute rent for any lease year or month, including fixed steps, percentage bumps and CPI with a floor and cap. (`argus-baserent`)
4. Given building expenses and a lease structure (NNN, full-service gross, modified gross, base year, expense stop), the student can compute the tenant's reimbursement, including gross-up and caps. (`argus-recoveries`)
5. Given concessions, the student can compute the landlord's up-front cost and the net effective rent. (`argus-concessions`)
6. Given a renewal probability and market assumptions, the student can compute blended downtime, TI, commissions, free rent and market rent at rollover, and the cash effect of an expiry. (`argus-mla`)
7. Given a cash flow, the student can separate turnover vacancy, general vacancy and credit loss and avoid counting vacancy twice. (`argus-vacancy`)
8. Given an ARGUS-style cash flow or valuation report, the student can find a line, explain a dip, and predict the direction and rough size of a change in an input. (`argus-reports`)
9. Given tenant inputs, the student can produce a five-year lease-by-lease cash flow in the simulator and in the grid, and reconcile the two. (`argus-simulator`, `argus-case`)

## 4. Key concepts, definitions and formulas

Definitions the builder must use consistently:

- **Rentable vs. usable area.** Rent and recoveries are charged on rentable area. (Load factor arithmetic belongs to `plan-reading`; mention only.)
- **Lease year.** Counted from commencement. Year 1 has no escalation. A lease starting 1 April has lease years that straddle calendar years; reports are usually by calendar or fiscal year, so a calendar year can contain two rent levels.
- **Recovery structures.** NNN: tenant pays its share of all recoverable expenses. Full-service gross: tenant pays none. Modified gross: tenant pays some categories. Base year and expense stop: tenant pays its share of increases over a threshold.
- **Recoverable expenses** exclude capital items, leasing costs, debt service and (usually) costs specific to another tenant.
- **Below-the-line items.** TI, leasing commissions and capital reserves are deducted after NOI to reach cash flow before debt service.
- **Market leasing assumption (MLA).** The package of assumptions applied when a lease expires: market rent, term, renewal probability, downtime, free rent, TI, commissions, recovery structure.
- **Turnover vacancy** is produced by the model (downtime between leases). **General vacancy** is an allowance. **Credit loss** is non-payment by tenants in occupancy.

#### Example 1: Pro rata share and a triple-net recovery (`argus-ex-nnn`)

Pro rata share = tenant rentable area / building rentable area. Under a triple-net (NNN) lease the tenant reimburses its share of ALL recoverable operating expenses.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Building rentable area (SF) | `building_sf` | 60,000 |
| Tenant rentable area (SF) | `tenant_sf` | 12,000 |
| Recoverable operating expenses | `opex` | $780,000 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Pro rata share | `share` | `=tenant_sf/building_sf` | 20.0% |
| NNN recovery | `recovery` | `=opex*share` | $156,000 |
| Recovery per tenant SF | `recovery_psf` | `=recovery/tenant_sf` | $13.00 |

*Note.* Recovery per SF equals building expenses per SF (opex / building_sf). That identity is a quick check.

#### Example 2: Base-year lease (`argus-ex-base-year`)

The landlord pays expenses up to the amount of the base year (usually the first lease year). The tenant reimburses only its share of the INCREASE over the base year, and never less than zero.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Pro rata share | `share` | 20.0% |
| Operating expenses this year | `opex` | $780,000 |
| Base-year operating expenses | `base_year` | $690,000 |
| Expenses in a year they fall | `opex_low` | $650,000 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Recovery this year | `recovery` | `=MAX(0,opex-base_year)*share` | $18,000 |
| Recovery in the low year | `recovery_low` | `=MAX(0,opex_low-base_year)*share` | $0 |
| Landlord keeps paying (this tenant’s share of the base) | `landlord_cost` | `=base_year*share` | $138,000 |

#### Example 3: Expense stop (`argus-ex-expense-stop`)

A stop is a fixed dollar amount per SF. The tenant reimburses expenses per SF above the stop, times its own area. It is a base year expressed as a negotiated number rather than an actual year.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Building rentable area (SF) | `building_sf` | 60,000 |
| Tenant rentable area (SF) | `tenant_sf` | 12,000 |
| Operating expenses this year | `opex` | $780,000 |
| Expense stop ($/SF) | `stop` | $12.00 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Expenses per SF | `opex_psf` | `=opex/building_sf` | $13.00 |
| Excess over the stop | `excess_psf` | `=MAX(0,opex_psf-stop)` | $1.00 |
| Recovery | `recovery` | `=excess_psf*tenant_sf` | $12,000 |

#### Example 4: Grossing up variable expenses (`argus-ex-gross-up`)

Variable expenses (janitorial, utilities, management) fall when a building is partly empty. Leases let the landlord "gross up" variable expenses to a stated occupancy (often 95%) so that occupied tenants pay a fair share and a low-occupancy base year is not locked in. Fixed expenses are not grossed up.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Fixed expenses (taxes, insurance) | `fixed` | $480,000 |
| Variable expenses at actual occupancy | `variable` | $300,000 |
| Actual occupancy | `occ` | 80% |
| Gross-up occupancy in the lease | `gross_occ` | 95% |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Variable expenses, grossed up | `variable_grossed` | `=variable*gross_occ/occ` | $356,250 |
| Expenses used for recoveries | `total_grossed` | `=fixed+variable_grossed` | $836,250 |
| Increase from the gross-up | `uplift` | `=total_grossed-(fixed+variable)` | $56,250 |

*Note.* A common bug is grossing up the fixed expenses too.

#### Example 5: Fixed annual escalations (`argus-ex-escalation`)

With fixed percentage steps, rent in lease year n = year-1 rent x (1 + step)^(n - 1). Year 1 has no escalation.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Tenant area (SF) | `tenant_sf` | 12,000 |
| Year-1 base rent ($/SF/yr) | `rent` | $36.00 |
| Annual escalation | `step` | 3.0% |
| Lease year wanted | `yr` | 6 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Rent per SF in that year | `rent_psf` | `=rent*(1+step)^(yr-1)` | $41.73 |
| Annual base rent | `annual_rent` | `=tenant_sf*rent_psf` | $500,806 |
| TRAP: one escalation too many | `wrong_extra_step` | `=tenant_sf*rent*(1+step)^yr` | $515,831 |

#### Example 6: Free rent, TI, commissions and net effective rent (`argus-ex-ner`)

Net effective rent (NER) spreads the landlord’s concessions over the lease term: (total base rent - free rent - tenant improvements - leasing commissions) / term, per SF per year. This simple version is undiscounted and uses flat rent. Convention used here: the commission is paid on rent actually payable (after free rent).

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Face rent ($/SF/yr, flat) | `rent` | $36.00 |
| Term (years) | `term_yrs` | 5 |
| Free rent (months) | `free_mo` | 4 |
| Tenant improvement allowance ($/SF) | `ti` | $40.00 |
| Leasing commission (% of rent payable) | `lc_pct` | 6% |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Face rent over the term ($/SF) | `gross_rent` | `=rent*term_yrs` | $180.00 |
| Free rent ($/SF) | `free_value` | `=rent*free_mo/12` | $12.00 |
| Rent actually payable ($/SF) | `payable` | `=gross_rent-free_value` | $168.00 |
| Leasing commission ($/SF) | `lc` | `=lc_pct*payable` | $10.08 |
| Net effective rent ($/SF/yr) | `ner` | `=(payable-ti-lc)/term_yrs` | $23.58 |
| NER as a share of face rent | `ner_share` | `=ner/rent` | 65.5% |

#### Example 7: Blended market leasing assumptions (`argus-ex-mla`)

At lease expiry the tenant either renews (probability p) or leaves. Lease-by-lease models blend the two outcomes: blended value = p x renewal value + (1 - p) x new-tenant value. Downtime applies only if the tenant leaves, so blended downtime = (1 - p) x downtime.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Renewal probability | `renew_prob` | 65% |
| Months vacant if the tenant leaves | `downtime_new` | 9 |
| TI for a new tenant ($/SF) | `ti_new` | $45.00 |
| TI on renewal ($/SF) | `ti_renew` | $15.00 |
| Commission, new lease | `lc_new` | 6% |
| Commission, renewal | `lc_renew` | 3% |
| Free rent, new lease (months) | `free_new` | 6 |
| Free rent, renewal (months) | `free_renew` | 2 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Blended downtime (months) | `downtime` | `=(1-renew_prob)*downtime_new` | 3.15 |
| Blended TI ($/SF) | `ti` | `=renew_prob*ti_renew+(1-renew_prob)*ti_new` | $25.50 |
| Blended commission | `lc` | `=renew_prob*lc_renew+(1-renew_prob)*lc_new` | 4.05% |
| Blended free rent (months) | `free` | `=renew_prob*free_renew+(1-renew_prob)*free_new` | 3.40 |

*Note.* The classic slip is weighting by the renewal probability the wrong way round (p x new + (1 - p) x renewal).

#### Example 8: Cost of one lease rolling (`argus-ex-rollover`)

Apply the blended assumptions to the expiring space: lost rent during downtime, tenant improvements and a commission on the new lease’s rent.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Expiring space (SF) | `tenant_sf` | 10,000 |
| Market rent at expiry ($/SF/yr) | `market_rent` | $42.00 |
| Blended downtime (months) | `downtime` | 3.15 |
| Blended TI ($/SF) | `ti` | $25.50 |
| Blended commission | `lc` | 4.05% |
| New lease term (years) | `new_term` | 5 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Rent lost to downtime | `lost_rent` | `=tenant_sf*market_rent*downtime/12` | $110,250 |

| Leasing commission | `lc_cost` | `=lc*tenant_sf*market_rent*new_term` | $85,050 |

#### Example 9: General vacancy, turnover vacancy and credit loss (`argus-ex-vacancy`)

Turnover vacancy is the downtime the model already projects lease by lease. General vacancy is a market-wide allowance (a % of potential gross revenue). To avoid counting vacancy twice, the general allowance is reduced by the turnover vacancy already in the cash flow, and never below zero. Credit loss (tenants who occupy but do not pay) is a separate line.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Potential gross revenue | `pgr` | $2,400,000 |
| Turnover vacancy already modelled | `turnover` | $96,000 |
| General vacancy allowance | `general_pct` | 5% |
| Credit loss | `credit_pct` | 1% |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| General vacancy target | `allowance` | `=general_pct*pgr` | $120,000 |
| General vacancy line (net of turnover) | `general` | `=MAX(0,allowance-turnover)` | $24,000 |
| Credit loss | `credit` | `=credit_pct*pgr` | $24,000 |
| Effective gross revenue | `egr` | `=pgr-turnover-general-credit` | $2,256,000 |
| TRAP: full allowance on top of turnover | `double_count` | `=pgr-turnover-allowance-credit` | $2,160,000 |

#### Example 10: Percentage rent with a natural breakpoint (`argus-ex-percentage-rent`)

Retail leases often add percentage rent: a share of the tenant’s sales above a breakpoint. The natural breakpoint is base rent / percentage, the sales level at which the percentage alone would equal base rent.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Annual base rent | `base_rent` | $180,000 |
| Percentage rent rate | `pct` | 6% |
| Tenant sales this year | `sales` | $3,400,000 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Natural breakpoint | `breakpoint` | `=base_rent/pct` | $3,000,000 |
| Percentage rent | `overage` | `=MAX(0,sales-breakpoint)*pct` | $24,000 |
| Total rent | `total_rent` | `=base_rent+overage` | $204,000 |

#### Example 11: Reading the valuation page: PV of cash flow plus resale (`argus-ex-valuation`)

A lease-by-lease valuation discounts each year’s cash flow and a resale. The resale capitalises the NOI of the year AFTER the hold (year 6 for a 5-year hold) at the exit cap rate, less selling costs.

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Year 1 cash flow before debt | `cf_a` | $1,150,000 |
| Year 2 | `cf_b` | $1,210,000 |
| Year 3 | `cf_c` | $980,000 |
| Year 4 | `cf_d` | $1,290,000 |
| Year 5 | `cf_e` | $1,340,000 |
| Year 6 NOI | `noi_next` | $1,420,000 |
| Exit cap rate | `exit_cap` | 6.50% |
| Selling costs | `sale_cost` | 2% |
| Discount rate | `disc` | 8.00% |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Gross resale value | `gross_resale` | `=noi_next/exit_cap` | $21,846,154 |
| Net resale proceeds | `net_resale` | `=gross_resale*(1-sale_cost)` | $21,409,231 |
| Present value | `pv` | `=NPV(disc,cf_a,cf_b,cf_c,cf_d,cf_e+net_resale)` | $19,311,083 |
| Share of value from the resale | `resale_share` | `=net_resale/(1+disc)^5/pv` | 75.5% |

*Note.* Year 3 dips because a large lease rolls (downtime, TI and commissions). Spotting why a year dips is a core report-reading skill.

Further formulas to cover (write each as a worked example in `spec-data.ts` before building questions on it):

- CPI escalation with floor and cap: `=rent*(1+MIN(cap,MAX(floor,cpi)))`.
- Controllable expense cap, cumulative vs. non-cumulative.
- WALT by rent: `=SUMPRODUCT(remaining_years, annual_rent)/SUM(annual_rent)`.
- Renewal rent as a share of market, blended: `=p*renew_pct*market + (1-p)*market`.
- Discounted NER: PV of all landlord cash flows, converted to a level annual payment with `PMT`.

## 5. Common misconceptions

Use these as distractors, `mistakes` and error-hunt bugs. Each line is "wrong idea → correction".

1. Base-year tenants reimburse all expenses → only the increase over the base year.
2. A recovery can be negative when expenses fall → it floors at zero.
3. The base year is subtracted after taking the tenant's share → subtract at building level, then apply the share (equivalent only if done consistently; the bug is subtracting the whole building's base from one tenant's share).
4. Gross-up applies to all expenses → variable expenses only.
5. The stop is compared with total dollars → it is per SF.
6. Escalation applies in year 1 → first escalation is at the start of year 2.
7. Simple vs. compound escalations confused.
8. Free rent reduces NOI forever → it is a one-time concession in the months it applies.
9. TI and commissions are operating expenses → they are below NOI.
10. Blended downtime = full downtime → multiply by (1 - renewal probability).
11. Renewal probability weights reversed.
12. General vacancy added on top of turnover vacancy → general is an allowance net of turnover (when the model is set that way; say which convention the question uses).
13. Credit loss is the same as vacancy → the space is occupied; the rent is not collected.
14. Resale uses the final year's NOI → it uses the NOI of the year after the hold.
15. Pro rata share uses usable area, or leased area as the denominator, without the lease saying so.
16. Percentage rent is charged on all sales → only on sales above the breakpoint.

## 8. Error hunts and spot-the-problem scenarios

1. Rent schedule where the first escalation is applied in year 1 (timing).
2. Recovery schedule where one tenant's base year is subtracted after the share is taken, and another's recovery goes negative (wrong-base, function).
3. Gross-up applied to fixed expenses (wrong-base).
4. MLA table with the renewal probability applied to the new-tenant column (concept).
5. Cash flow with general vacancy charged in full on top of turnover vacancy, and TI included in operating expenses (double-count, concept).
6. Docreview: a rent roll whose annual rent column does not equal SF x rent/SF for one suite, an expiry before a start date, and a suite listed twice.
7. Docreview: a lease abstract that contradicts the clause text on escalation date, recovery structure and renewal option notice period.
8. Docreview: a valuation page whose resale uses the final year's NOI.

## 9. Case study brief and rubric

**`argus-case-...` (type `timed`, level 3, about 40 minutes, pass mark 80%).**

*Brief.* A 48,000 SF two-storey suburban office building in an illustrative San Diego submarket has five tenants and one vacant suite. The student receives the rent roll (on a second sheet), building expenses and one MLA. Deliverables, all on the first sheet:

1. Year 1 to 5 base rent by tenant, including one tenant that expires in year 3 and re-leases under the MLA.
2. Recoveries by tenant (at least one NNN, one base year, one stop).
3. Turnover vacancy, general vacancy net of turnover, credit loss.
4. NOI, then TI, commissions and reserves to cash flow before debt.

### 13.1 Lease engine: `src/leases/engine.ts` (no UI code)

```ts
export type RecoveryType = 'nnn' | 'gross' | 'base-year' | 'stop';

export interface Tenant {
  id: string;            // "suite-200"
  name: string;
  sf: number;
  startYear: number;     // analysis years are 1..N; a lease in place has startYear <= 1
  endYear: number;       // last year of the current lease (inclusive)
  baseRent: number;      // $/SF/yr in the tenant's first lease year
  escalation: number;    // annual %, compounding
  recovery: RecoveryType;
  baseYearExpenses?: number; // building total, for 'base-year'
  stopPsf?: number;          // for 'stop'
}

export interface MarketAssumptions {
  marketRent: number;      // $/SF/yr in year 1
  marketGrowth: number;    // annual %
  renewalProbability: number;
  downtimeMonths: number;  // if the tenant leaves
  termYears: number;
  tiNew: number; tiRenew: number;   // $/SF
  lcNew: number; lcRenew: number;   // % of first-term rent
  freeNew: number; freeRenew: number; // months
  recovery: RecoveryType;  // structure of speculative leases (base year resets to the rollover year)
}

export interface Building {
  sf: number;
  expensesYear1: number;   // recoverable, building total
  expenseGrowth: number;
  generalVacancy: number;  // allowance, % of PGR, net of turnover vacancy
  creditLoss: number;      // % of PGR
  reserves: number;        // $/SF/yr, below NOI
}

export interface CashFlowYear {
  year: number;
  baseRent: number; recoveries: number; freeRent: number;      // freeRent <= 0
  turnoverVacancy: number; generalVacancy: number; creditLoss: number; // <= 0
  effectiveGrossRevenue: number; operatingExpenses: number; noi: number;
  tenantImprovements: number; leasingCommissions: number; reserves: number;
  cashFlowBeforeDebt: number;
  byTenant: Record<string, { baseRent: number; recovery: number; status: 'in-place' | 'downtime' | 'speculative' }>;
}

export function projectCashFlow(building: Building, tenants: Tenant[], mla: MarketAssumptions, years: number): CashFlowYear[];
export function valuation(cf: CashFlowYear[], discount: number, exitCap: number, saleCost: number): { pv: number; resale: number };
export function facts(cf: CashFlowYear[]): Record<string, number>; // e.g. noi_year_three, recoveries_year_one: names valid as formula variables
```

Simplifications to state in the component's help text: annual periods; downtime and free rent are prorated within the rollover year; one MLA for the whole building; one rollover per suite inside the analysis period is enough (a second is welcome if tested).

Tests (`tests/leases.test.ts`): every worked example in section 4 reproduced through the engine; a building with all-NNN tenants recovers exactly 100% of expenses when full; renewal probability 1 gives no downtime; probability 0 gives full downtime; revenue lines sum; randomized inputs (30 seeds) keep NOI = EGR - expenses and cash flow = NOI - TI - LC - reserves to the cent.
