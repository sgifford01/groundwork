## 1. Purpose and career fit

A developer does not swing a hammer, but signs the construction contract, approves every payment and owns the budget. An analyst is expected to read a development budget, know what a GMP is, track contingency, review a pay application and say what a three-month delay costs. This short category covers that vocabulary and arithmetic, enough to follow an owner-architect-contractor meeting and to keep a pro forma's cost side honest.

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Given a development budget, the student can classify lines as land, hard or soft cost and compute cost per SF and per unit. (`construction-costs`)
2. Given an estimate stage, the student can explain why contingency differs and how the estimate tightens. (`construction-costs`)
3. Given a project situation, the student can choose between design-bid-build, design-build and CM at risk, and between lump sum, cost-plus and GMP, and say who carries which risk. (`construction-delivery`)
4. Given change orders and progress, the student can price a change, track contingency and judge whether it is being used too fast. (`construction-changes`)
5. Given a pay application, the student can compute retainage and the amount due and spot overbilling, front-loading and unsupported stored materials. (`construction-draws`)
6. Given task durations and dependencies, the student can find the critical path and float; given a delay, its cost. (`construction-schedule`)

## 4. Key concepts, definitions and formulas

- **Hard costs:** the physical work, including the contractor's general conditions, overhead and fee. **Soft costs:** design, permits, legal, insurance, financing costs, developer fee. **Land** is a third bucket.
- **Contingency:** a budget allowance for the unknown. Owner's contingency sits in the development budget; contractor's contingency sits inside the contract sum.
- **Delivery method:** who is hired, when, and who holds whose contract. **Contract type:** how the price is set.
- **GMP:** cost of the work plus fee, capped. **Lump sum:** fixed price. **Cost-plus:** actual cost plus a fee, uncapped.
- **Change order:** a signed change to the contract's scope, price or time.
- **Schedule of values:** the contract sum broken into lines, used to bill by percent complete.
- **Retainage:** a percentage of each payment held until the work is finished.
- **Critical path:** the longest chain of dependent tasks. **Float:** how far a task can slip without delaying completion.

| Final cost in an overrun case | `overrun_case` | $30,900,000 |

|---|---|---|---:|
| Savings | `savings` | `=MAX(0,gmp-cost_plus_fee)` | $800,000 |

## 5. Common misconceptions

1. Architect and permit fees are hard costs → soft.
2. The contractor's fee and general conditions are soft costs → hard (they are part of the construction contract).
3. Contingency is profit or slack → it is expected to be spent on real unknowns.
4. Contingency is measured against total cost in a drawdown check → against the contingency itself, and against work remaining.
5. A GMP means the owner pays the GMP → the owner pays cost plus fee up to the GMP; savings are shared.
6. Lump sum is always cheapest → the contractor prices the risk it takes.
7. Design-build means the builder designs for free → one contract covers both; the owner trades control for speed and a single point of responsibility.
8. A change order only changes price → it can change time too.
9. Retainage is a fee kept by the owner → it is the contractor's money, paid at completion.
10. Percent billed should track the calendar → it tracks work in place.
11. Stored materials are always billable → only with proof of ownership, insurance and access.
12. Any delayed task delays the project → only if it is on the critical path or uses up its float.
13. Delay costs only interest → also time-related costs and deferred income.
14. Equity and loan fund side by side from day one → lenders normally require equity in first.
