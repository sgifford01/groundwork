## 1. Purpose and career fit

A development analyst is handed a plan set in the first week and is expected to find the unit count, check the parking, measure a setback and notice that the pro forma's unit mix does not match the drawings. Nobody teaches this formally. This category teaches reading 2D drawings the way a paper set is read (sheet index → sheet → callout → detail), then turns what is on the page into the numbers a pro forma starts from: gross and rentable area, efficiency, unit mix, parking count.

It is the visual counterpart of the Excel course. It applies zoning numbers in test fits but does not teach zoning (that is `entitlements`), and it does not price construction (`construction`).

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Given a plan set, the student can use the sheet index and discipline prefixes to open the sheet that answers a stated question. (`plan-reading-set`)
2. Given a title block and revision marks, the student can state the sheet number, scale, and what changed in the latest revision. (`plan-reading-set`)
3. Given a drawing, the student can name its type (site plan, floor plan, roof plan, section, elevation, enlarged plan, wall section, detail, reflected ceiling plan) and say what question it answers. (`plan-reading-types`)
4. Given a symbol or annotation, the student can name it and locate examples on a sheet; given a callout, the student can state the sheet and drawing it refers to. (`plan-reading-symbols`)
5. Given a scale, the student can convert a paper measurement to a real length within 2% and compute an area. (`plan-reading-scale`)
6. Given a floor plan of an apartment, retail, office or mixed-use building, the student can identify its parts (core, corridor, units, storefront, lease depth, podium, drive aisle). (`plan-reading-buildings`)
7. Given a plan, the student can compute GSF, net or rentable area, efficiency, load factor, unit mix, average unit size and parking count. (`plan-reading-program`)
8. Given a plan and a stated requirement, the student can spot a problem: a missing second exit, a parking shortfall, a unit mix that does not match the stated program. (`plan-reading-program`, `plan-reading-buildings`)
9. Given a plan, the student can take off units and areas and build a mini pro forma from them in the grid. (`plan-reading-bridge`)

What each subtopic must contain:

- **`plan-reading-set` (8):** cover sheet; sheet index; discipline designators G, C, L, S, A, F, P, M, E (the U.S. National CAD Standard uses F for fire protection; some firms write FP) and the sheet-type digit that follows (1 plans, 2 elevations, 3 sections, 4 large-scale views, 5 details, 6 schedules; say "commonly", firms vary). This app's drawings follow that numbering: C-101 site plan, A-102 floor plan, A-301 section, A-401 enlarged plan; title block fields; revision clouds and deltas; "do not scale drawings". Types: `plan` navigate and identify tasks on G-001 and title blocks, flashcards, 1 order ("put these sheets in set order").
- **`plan-reading-types` (9):** what each drawing type is cut or projected from and what it answers. Needs the new sheets from Chunk 2. Types: `plan` identify ("what type of drawing is this sheet?"), mcq ("which sheet would show the floor-to-floor height?"), flashcards.
- **`plan-reading-symbols` (12, 1 seed):** north arrow, graphic scale, grid lines and bubbles, dimension strings, section cut markers, elevation markers, detail and enlarged-plan callouts, room/door/window tags, door swings, stairs (UP/DN), elevators, plumbing fixtures, property lines, setback lines, easements, centre lines. Mostly `plan` locate and identify.
- **`plan-reading-scale` (9):** architectural scales (1/8", 1/4" = 1'-0"), engineering scales (1" = 20', 1" = 40'), graphic scale, feet-and-inches arithmetic, measuring with the ruler in `paper` mode, areas, why written dimensions govern. Types: `plan` measure, numeric, 1 estimate-style numeric.
- **`plan-reading-buildings` (12):** multifamily (double-loaded corridor, unit plans, cores, two means of egress), ground-floor retail (storefront, frontage, depth, back-of-house, loading), office plates (core, lease depth, column spacing), mixed-use podium and wrap, parking (stall, aisle, module, ramp, accessible stalls shown by symbol). - **`plan-reading-program` (11, 1 seed):** GSF/GBA vs. NRA/NSF vs. rentable; efficiency; load factor; unit mix; average unit size; parking count and ratio; test fits against given limits; spot-the-problem.

## 4. Key concepts, definitions and formulas

- **GSF / GBA (gross):** everything inside the exterior walls on all floors. **NRA / NSF (net rentable / net saleable):** the space that earns rent. **Efficiency** = net / gross. Typical illustrative ranges: corridor apartments 78% to 88%, office floors 82% to 90%.
- **Rentable vs. usable (office):** usable is the tenant's own space; rentable adds a share of common areas. **Load factor** = rentable / usable.
- **Dimensions** in this app's drawings are to wall centrelines and say so on the sheet. Written dimensions govern over scaled measurements.
- **Scale:** architectural = fraction of an inch per foot; engineering = feet per inch.
- **Egress basics (conceptual only):** apartments off a corridor normally need two separate exits; that is why stairs sit at opposite ends. Do not quote code distances or occupant loads.

#### Example 1: Architectural scale (`plan-ex-arch-scale`)

An architectural scale is written as inches on paper = one foot, e.g. 1/8" = 1'-0". Real feet = inches measured x feet per inch, where feet per inch is the reciprocal of the fraction (8 for 1/8", 4 for 1/4").

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Measured on paper (inches) | `paper_in` | 3.25 |
| Feet per inch at 1/8" = 1'-0" | `ft_per_in` | 8 |
| Feet per inch at 1/4" = 1'-0" | `ft_per_in_quarter` | 4 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Real length at 1/8" scale (ft) | `real_ft` | `=paper_in*ft_per_in` | 26.0 |
| Same measurement on a 1/4" drawing (ft) | `real_ft_quarter` | `=paper_in*ft_per_in_quarter` | 13.0 |

*Note.* A larger fraction is a larger-scale (more zoomed-in) drawing, so the same paper length is a shorter real distance.

#### Example 2: Engineering (civil) scale (`plan-ex-eng-scale`)

| Lot depth on paper (inches) | `depth_in` | 6.00 |
| Scale: 1" = this many feet | `scale` | 20 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Lot width (ft) | `width_ft` | `=width_in*scale` | 90 |
| Lot depth (ft) | `depth_ft` | `=depth_in*scale` | 120 |

| Given | Name | Value |
|---|---|---:|

| Floor usable area (SF) | `floor_usable` | 17,000 |
| Tenant usable area (SF) | `tenant_usable` | 5,000 |

| Step | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Load factor | `load_factor` | `=floor_rentable/floor_usable` | 1.18 |
| Tenant rentable area (SF) | `tenant_rentable` | `=tenant_usable*load_factor` | 5,882 |

## 5. Common misconceptions

1. Corridor, stairs and elevator count as rentable → they are common area.
2. Average unit size is the average of the unit types → it is weighted by count.
3. Efficiency = gross / net (inverted).
4. A section marker is confused with a detail callout or grid bubble → a section marker has a cut line and a direction arrow; a grid bubble sits on a centre line and holds one number or letter; a callout bubble is split, drawing number over sheet number.
5. The bottom half of a callout bubble is the detail number → the bottom is the SHEET, the top is the drawing on that sheet.
6. 1/4" scale is "smaller" than 1/8" → it is larger (more detail, less area per sheet).
7. Scaling an area by the linear factor once instead of squared.
8. Reading an engineering scale as architectural (1" = 20' read as 1/20" = 1').
9. A door swing arc is a curved wall.
10. An elevator (box with an X) is taken for a shaft/duct or a stair.
11. Plan north vs. true north ignored; "up the page" assumed to be north without checking the arrow.
12. Units are counted from tags on one floor and not multiplied by floors, or the ground floor (retail, lobby) is assumed to repeat.
13. Parking stalls counted without subtracting columns, ramps or accessible aisles where shown.
14. Property line, setback line and building line confused.
15. Rentable area and usable area treated as the same thing on an office floor.

## 8. Error hunts and spot-the-problem scenarios

1. Building program sheet that counts the corridor as rentable and averages unit sizes without weighting (grid error hunt).
2. Parking table that rounds required stalls down and omits a floor of units (grid error hunt).
3. Plan with one stair removed by a parameter (`stairs: 1`): "what is wrong with this floor?" → click the dead-end of the corridor (needs a generator parameter and an element with role `egress-problem`).
4. Parking level whose stall count is below the given requirement: compute the shortfall (measure task) and a locate task on the row that could be restriped.
5. Cover sheet project data (docreview, rendered from plan facts) where the unit count, GSF or mix disagrees with the drawings: the student must check against A-102.
6. Title block scale that disagrees with the graphic scale on the same sheet.

## 9. Case study brief and rubric

**`plan-reading-case-...` (level 3, about 35 minutes, pass mark 80%): a `plangrid` with a timer (section 13.3, "Still to build"), so the plan set sits above the sheet.**

*Brief.* An illustrative five-storey mixed-use building: ground-floor retail and lobby over a parking level, four apartment floors above. The student is given the plan set (cover, site plan, parking plan, ground floor, typical floor, enlarged unit, section) and a short list of given limits (FAR, required parking ratio, target unit mix). Deliverables in the grid:

1. Takeoff: units by type per floor and in total; retail area; stalls.
2. Program: GSF by floor and total, residential NRA, efficiency, average unit size, FAR used.
3. Checks: parking required vs. provided; unit mix vs. target; FAR vs. limit (each as a pass/fail formula).
4. Mini pro forma: residential and retail revenue, NOI and value from given rents and rates.

*Rubric (auto-graded).* Takeoff cells 30% (value-checked against plan facts), program formulas 35%, checks 15%, pro forma 20%. A `plangrid` counts every cell equally, so meet these weights by the number of cells in each part rather than by new scoring code. `mistakes` must cover: corridor counted as rentable, ground floor multiplied as a typical floor, unweighted average size, stalls rounded down, retail rent applied per month instead of per year.

### 13.2 Data model rules for new generators

- One file per plan set in `src/plans/`, exporting a `PlanSetDef` and registered in `PLAN_SETS`. A new SHEET for the apartment set is a new function in `mfDoubleLoaded.ts`, added to the `drawings` list in `build()` (the cover index then lists it automatically).
- Sheet numbers follow the convention the flashcards teach: discipline letter, then sheet type (1 plans, 2 elevations, 3 sections, 4 large-scale views, 5 details).
- Every number a question could ask for is a `fact`, named in lowercase_with_underscores (no digits), computed from the parameters, and cross-checked in `tests/plans.test.ts` against the drawn geometry (sum of room areas = gross area; counts of elements by role = count facts; dimension strings as long as the facts they state).
- Every drawn thing a question could ask about is its own element with a stable `role`. Add roles as needed (for example `elevation-marker, stall, accessible-stall, drive-aisle, ramp, column, storefront, loading, retail-bay, door-tag, window-tag`) and list them in the comment at the top of the generator.
- An element whose outline is a line (a property line, a setback line) needs a thin band as its `hit`, one element per segment, so that clicking inside the lot does not count as clicking the line.
- Nothing visible on a sheet may answer a question asked about that sheet: not a room label, not a general note, not the project name, not the cover subtitle (the stairs are marked UP/DN, not "STAIR"; setback lines are dimensioned but not labelled; the loading bay is unlabelled and the note only says where the alley is; the podium slab is labelled "CONCRETE SLAB" and the project is not called a podium). Element `name`s are fine: they stay hidden until the question is graded. Before committing a question, list the text on its sheets and read it with the question in mind.
- Parameters have `limits`; every question's `variants` must lie inside them (enforced at load). Type variant lists as `Record<string, number>[]`.

### 13.4 Plan to spreadsheet: the `plangrid` type (built in Chunk 4)

**What changed from the original plan, and why.** This section first proposed a `figure` field and a new `takeoff` cell kind on the core sheet types (`grid`, `timed`, `case`). That would have changed code the Excel & Pro Forma course depends on. Instead, Chunk 4 built a separate category-only type, `plangrid`, that reuses the core grid grader without changing it. The core sheet types, their screens and their tests are untouched.

**Fields.** Everything a `grid` has (`workbook`, `mistakes`, `edgeCases`, `tolerance`), plus:

- `figure: { set, variants, sheet }`: the plan set, its parameter variants (the attempt's seed picks one) and the sheet shown first.
- `takeoffs[]` (1 to 8): `{ cell, sheet?, label, answer, format, tolerance, vary?, mistakes[] }`. `answer` is a formula over the plan's facts (`=one_bed_count`); `mistakes` are numeric mistakes over the same facts (`=bays` for "counted one side of the corridor only"). A takeoff cell must not also be a cell of the workbook. Counts take `tolerance: { abs: 0.4 }` so they must be exact; other numbers default to 0.5%.

**How it is graded** (`src/grading/plangrid.ts`, no UI code):

1. `figureFor(item, seed)` builds the drawing for the attempt.
2. Two versions of the workbook are made from it. In the **key** version each takeoff cell is a blue input holding the right number from the drawing, varied in the stress test like any input (`vary` sets the range). In the **display** version each takeoff cell is a yellow cell the student types into.
3. The formula cells are graded by the unchanged `gradeGrid` against the key version. It ignores anything typed into an input cell, so formula cells are graded as if every takeoff were right: a wrong takeoff costs only its own cell. Because takeoff inputs are varied like any input, a count typed into a formula (`=5*B9`) fails the stress test.
4. Each takeoff is graded on its own like a numeric answer over the facts: empty, not a number, a listed mistake (named), or wrong. A typed number or a formula that gives the number both count.
5. Score = (formula-cell credit + correct takeoffs) / (formula cells + takeoffs); every cell counts equally.

**Screen** (`components/questions/PlanGridQuestion.tsx`): the plan viewer (with the Measure tool) above the grid; the prompt and explanation can use `{fact}` templates; feedback shows a "Numbers from the drawing" table and a "Formula cells" table. There is no .xlsx download for this type, because the drawing only exists in the app.

**Tests** (`tests/category-content.test.ts`, "plan to spreadsheet"), for every `plangrid`, most of them for every variant of its drawing: the item loads and its text renders; every takeoff feeds at least one formula cell; the answer key is clean under random inputs; the model answer scores 100% and a blank sheet 0; each takeoff mistake is caught by name and costs only its own cell; a typed count inside a formula fails the stress test; typed numbers in formula cells are caught as hardcoding; each grid mistake is caught and named.

### 13.5 Grading API (keep UI-free, in `src/grading/category.ts`)

`planFor(item, seed) → PlanSet`; `gradeLocate(item, set, key | null) → { correct, message, category, picked }` and `gradeLocateMany(item, set, keys[])`, where a key is `"<sheet>/<element id>"`; `gradeMeasure(item, set, typed) → NumericResult`; `identifyOptions(item, rng)`; `navigateOptions(item, set, rng)`. The plan-to-spreadsheet grading is in its own file, `src/grading/plangrid.ts`: `figureFor`, `gradePlanGrid(item, set, attempt, opts)`, `modelAttempt` and `planGridProblems` (the load-time checks).

### 13.6 Accessibility

- The SVG is focusable; arrow keys pan, + and - zoom, 0 fits (built).
- Every hotspot is a focusable `role="button"`. Before grading its label is neutral ("Drawing element 12") so that screen-reader users are not handed the answer; after grading it is the element's real name (built). Tab order should follow reading order (left to right, top to bottom): sort hit elements by position when rendering.
- Add a "List elements" toggle: a visually hidden-by-default list of the clickable elements on the sheet, each as a button labelled by neutral position ("element near grid 3 / B"), so the task is completable without a pointer.
- The Measure tool must work from the keyboard: Tab to a snap point list, Enter to place.
- Touch: one-finger drag pans, two-finger pinch zooms (track two pointers; `touch-action: none` is already set). Hotspots must be at least 24 px at fit zoom or zoom-to-target on focus.
- Colour is never the only signal: selected, right and wrong states also differ in stroke pattern or carry a ✓/✗ marker.
- Plan questions are in `DESKTOP_ONLY_TYPES`, so phone-friendly sessions skip them.
